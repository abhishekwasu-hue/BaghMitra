## Critical stages and the bahar rest (both crops unless marked)

- Water a few days before flowering and again after fruit set; a shortage at these stages sheds flowers
  and young fruit [C06].
- Water at fruit maturity too, to avoid shrinkage and dry pulp; mild late stress raises TSS but makes
  fruit smaller [C06].
- Summer is the critical period: frequent light irrigation lowers soil temperature [C06].
- Mulch (dry leaves, paddy husk) about 8 cm thick in the basin reduces the number of irrigations [N1 §5.10].
- Bahar rest (santra): water is withheld for about a month before flowering, until leaves wilt; in
  light, sandy or shallow soils 2–3 weeks is enough [N1 §5.13]. For Mrig on medium soil PDKV found
  50 days of soil-moisture stress sufficient [P1]. A CCRI trial describes 30–45 days before expected
  flowering [S8]. Side by side: about 30 days [N1] / 30–45 days [S8] / 50 days (Mrig, medium soil) [P1].
- Mosambi bahar rest, side by side:
  - MPKV Rahuri (western Maharashtra, Ambia bahar): water stress in November–December, i.e. about two
    months [MP25 §8a].
  - By soil, Ambia bahar: light 35–45 days, medium 45–60 days, heavy 55–75 days; stress time
    November–December [AW24, Badnapur mosambi station]; the same soil table for santra and mosambi from
    Dr. PDKV [AW23].
  - Andhra Pradesh conditions (sweet orange): "Stress period of 20–25 days is sufficient" [VK].
  - Start and end the stress gradually, do not over-stress; on young trees, first mild stress in the 4th
    year [AW24].
- Rainy season: drain excess water; PDKV describes a drainage channel between rows (60 cm wide, 30 cm
  deep) in decline management [P1].
