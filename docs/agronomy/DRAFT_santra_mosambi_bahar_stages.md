# Bahar stage calendars for Nagpur mandarin (santra) and mosambi in Vidarbha: sourced values

Research date: 2026-10-12. Prepared for BaghMitra crop-stage template seeding.

## How this was gathered (read first)

- **No primary document could be opened.** The sandbox proxy refused every direct fetch: ccri.icar.gov.in, ccri.org.in, nhb.gov.in, pdkv.ac.in, krishi.icar.gov.in, researchgate, wikipedia and others all returned HTTP 403 or a DNS failure. Every value below therefore comes from **search-engine excerpts** of the cited documents. The excerpts quote the source text, but I could not check page numbers or the surrounding context.
- **Before seeding production data**, a person should open each primary PDF or URL, confirm the quoted sentence, and add a page number. The CCRI monthly advisories, the NHB orange report and the Ladaniya papers matter most.
- **Confidence labels used:**
  - **H**: the quote is attributed directly to an acceptable primary source (CCRI, NHB, ICAR journal, or a peer-reviewed paper).
  - **M**: a primary or peer-reviewed source, but the figure came through a secondary citation or a truncated excerpt.
  - **L**: an extension or press article (Agrowon, Vikaspedia, Lokmat). These are often written by SAU or CCRI scientists, but **I could not verify the authorship**, so they do not meet the spec's source list unless the author is confirmed.
- **Day offsets:** I give a day offset only where a source states one. Where a source gives only months, I give months. I have **not converted** months into day offsets myself, because that would be an invented number.

## 8. Which bahar for Vidarbha? (conflicting)

- **Mrig preferred (official):** NHB says water availability is a problem in central India during April–May, so farmers prefer Mrig bahar. NHB also says the Mrig crop maturing in February–March "has great potential for export since arrivals of mandarin fruit in international market are limited then" [S3] (H). A CCRI/APEDA initiative also ties Vidarbha export to the Mrig season [S4] (M).
- **Ambia preferred (field reports):** An Outlook India article quotes a farmer saying "Ambiya Bahar is best suited for Vidarbha", and a state agriculture officer saying growers lean towards Ambia [S31] (L, press). The IJLRA GI review says Ambia gives more reliable yields and holds up better against Jan–Mar hail damage [S17] (L). FreshPlaza says the Mrig crop "carries more risk because it depends on irrigation" when water is scarce [S32] (L, press).
- **Flavour and market:** Ambia fruit is more acidic (tangy) and Mrig fruit sweeter (several sources). Ambia reaches market from October; Mrig is marketed February–March [S32].
- **Conclusion:** official sources (NHB) favour Mrig for water and export reasons, while recent field reports say most Vidarbha growers take Ambia. The app should let growers choose either; it should not hard-code one as recommended.
